# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

People who record or link a meeting — professionals, small teams, students — and want the content back in a form they can scan and query instead of re-watching. They paste a YouTube URL or upload a file, then read the digest and ask follow-up questions. Sessions are short and task-focused: process a meeting, check the action items, ask one or two clarifying questions, move on.

## Product Purpose

Turn a raw meeting recording into a structured, searchable record. The backend already transcribes audio (Groq Whisper / Sarvam), extracts a title, summary, action items, key decisions, and open questions in one LLM pass, and builds a retrieval-augmented chat over the transcript. The frontend's job is to make that pipeline feel effortless: drop in a recording, see the digest appear, and keep asking questions. Success is a user who never has to open the original recording again.

## Positioning

A meeting recording becomes a structured, queryable record — not a video you have to sit through again.

## Brand Personality

Breathy, minimal, quietly spacious. The interface should feel like it has room to breathe, the way a night sky does: mostly calm dark space, a few points of light, nothing crowded. Unhurried and non-corporate — closer to a calm personal tool than an enterprise dashboard.

## Anti-references

Not neon cyberpunk sci-fi (no laser gradients, no "hacker terminal" glow). Not literal astronomy imagery (no planets, rockets, or NASA photography). Not a busy SaaS dashboard (no dense stat-tile grids or cluttered enterprise chrome). Not a copy of the layout reference's own color scheme — the reference is for structure only.

## Design Principles

Restraint over spectacle: the galaxy motif is a felt atmosphere, not a set piece — a handful of subtle stars, never a starfield you consciously notice. Content is the interface: transcripts, summaries, and chat messages carry the product; surrounding chrome stays quiet and out of the way. One accent, used deliberately: the brand purple (#A284E3) marks action and focus, not decoration, and should stay a minority of any surface. Dense information stays legible: action items, decisions, and open questions need real scan-ability, not stylistic flourish.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Sane defaults: WCAG AA contrast, keyboard-navigable, and every animation (including the starfield) respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. No formal compliance target beyond that.
